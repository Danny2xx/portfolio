## Register

## Users

Hiring managers, engineering leads, and technical recruiters evaluating Daniel for a
full-time **AI Engineer / Software Engineer / Full-Stack Developer** role. They arrive
skeptical and time-poor, often from a LinkedIn or referral link, frequently on mobile.
Their job-to-be-done: decide in under 60 seconds whether Daniel is worth a conversation,
then (if convinced) verify depth — real systems built, real decisions made, real impact.
A secondary audience is fellow engineers who may vouch for or share the work.

## Product Purpose

A single-page portfolio that converts a cold visitor into "let's talk." It exists to
prove technical range and judgment, not just list it. Success = the visitor reaches out
(email / LinkedIn) or leaves able to confidently recommend Daniel. The differentiator
versus a generic dev portfolio: the page itself is a working artifact of his engineering —
live, interactive AI/code demos embedded inline — so the medium is the proof.

## Brand Personality

Precise, technical, quietly confident. Three words: **rigorous, alive, unpretentious.**
Voice is an engineer's engineer — specific over salesy, shows the work, never inflates.
Confidence comes from depth and craft, not adjectives. Allows dry wit; never gimmicky.
The interface should feel *engineered* (exact spacing, intentional motion, fast) the way
his code is.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS / "AI made this" template**: cream/sand body bg, identical icon-card
  grids, hero-metric blocks, tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows over every section. Banned.
- **Cluttered dev resume**: skill-bar percentages, every tech logo crammed in a wall, no
  hierarchy, no breathing room.
- **Soulless corporate**: stock imagery, safe, interchangeable, no point of view.
- Reference site (aaezekiel.co) is the *structural* inspiration, NOT a template to clone —
  we match its ambition and aliveness, not its exact visuals or its designer-y playfulness.

## Design Principles

1. [ ] **The medium is the proof.** Don't claim "I build AI systems" — embed a working one.
    Every interactive element should be a real demonstration of competence, not decoration.
2. [ ] **Show, don't tell.** Specific systems, specific decisions, specific numbers over
    adjectives. Case studies read like engineering, not marketing.
3. [ ] **Engineered, not decorated.** Motion, spacing, and color are intentional and earn
    their place. If it doesn't aid comprehension or demonstrate craft, it's cut.
4. [ ] **Fast and accessible is a flex.** Performance and accessibility are themselves a
    demonstration of engineering discipline — treat them as features, not compliance.
5. [ ] **Skimmable in 60s, deep on demand.** A skeptical recruiter gets the verdict fast;
    a convinced one can drill into real depth.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, visible focus states, full
keyboard navigation, semantic landmarks, respects `prefers-reduced-motion` (every
animation has a reduced/instant alternative), and `prefers-color-scheme`. Accessibility
is positioned as evidence of engineering rigor, consistent with the reference's
accessibility-first stance.
